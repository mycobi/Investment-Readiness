# Overview

Terms and acronyms are defined in the [glossary](03-glossary.md).

## Purpose

Investment Readiness defines a shared vocabulary for evaluating whether a private company is prepared for institutional capital diligence. It is published so founders, investors, advisors, accelerators, and operators can discuss the same diligence domains in the same language.

## Audience

- Founders and executive teams preparing for diligence
- Investors and allocators comparing companies on a common scale
- Advisors and accelerators coaching portfolio companies
- Practitioners who want to improve the public criteria index

## Design principles

1. **Map, not verdict.** A low score is a development roadmap, not a permanent rejection label.
2. **Ontology over algorithm.** This standard names what is measured. It does not publish calibrated scoring instruments.
3. **Evidence over assertion.** Claims are stronger when supported by documents or system records.
4. **Investment readiness only.** The IRS does not measure technical or commercial maturity and does not fold either into the score.
5. **Industry-facing.** The vocabulary is meant to be adopted and improved beyond a single implementation.

## Related work

This standard builds on earlier readiness work and does not claim to be the first. Steve Blank's Investment Readiness Level (2013) set out a nine-level ladder and a weekly cycle of logged customer evidence. The KTH Innovation Readiness Level assesses an innovation on a one-to-nine scale across six dimensions, is publicly licensed, and is used at several universities. Village Capital's VIRAL framework maps nine levels against eight categories and was built from interviews with entrepreneurs and investors. The EEN2EIC Investment Readiness Scorecard, published in December 2025 as part of the Ready4Invest toolkit, offers an online self-assessment with an automated report and advisor follow-up. This specification differs mainly in its public criteria index and in its treatment of evidence provenance (see [methodology, section 6](01-methodology.md)).

## Diagnostic, score and public scope

The Investment Readiness Diagnostic (IRD) is the diagnostic; the Investment Readiness Score (IRS) is its numerical summary. This is a proposed public assessment vocabulary and reporting architecture, not a complete reproducible scoring algorithm.

Read [the glossary](03-glossary.md), [the public evidence boundary](04-public-scope-and-evidence.md), and [the review workflow](05-assessment-review-workflow.md). The framework's validity and financing effects remain subject to testing.
